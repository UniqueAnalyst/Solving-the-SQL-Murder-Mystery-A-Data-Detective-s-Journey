# 🕵️‍♂️ Solving the SQL Murder Mystery: A Data Detective's Journey

![SQL](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/de8b3b0f-f195-4fb3-a566-435fc4c92be1)


As a data analyst passionate about turning queries into insights, I recently participated in the **SQL Murder Mystery challenge** by Northwestern University Knight Lab during a class at **Vephla University**. This hands-on exercise sharpened our SQL skills while guiding us through a fictional crime investigation in SQL City.  

Together, we analyzed the crime scene, examined the evidence, and pieced the story together step by step until the culprit was revealed. It was an exciting mix of data storytelling, logical reasoning, and collaborative problem-solving.  

---

## 📊 Data Table Overview

Here’s a snapshot of the datasets we worked with:  

- **crime_scene_report** – Details of crimes by date, type, and city.  
- **person** – Personal information including ID, name, address, and SSN.  
- **drivers_license** – License details such as age, gender, vehicle, and plate number.  
- **facebook_event_checkin** – Event attendance records with person ID and dates.  
- **interview** – Interview transcripts from witnesses and suspects.  
- **get_fit_now_member** – Gym membership details and status.  
- **get_fit_now_checkin** – Gym visit logs with date and time.  
- **income** – Annual income information linked by SSN.  

---

## 🕰️ The Crime Scene

It was a cold January morning in SQL City when the crime occurred—**January 15, 2018**. A murder, right in the heart of the city.  

The police database held the clues, but the original crime scene report had mysteriously vanished. All that remained was a hazy memory: a murder in SQL City on that date. Diving into the records revealed the first thread of the story.  

Security footage captured part of the moment, and two witnesses had seen something:  

- **Morty Schapiro** 🏠 described hearing a sharp gunshot and watching a man flee carrying a distinctive **Get Fit Now Gym bag** with a membership number starting with "48Z." The man escaped in a car with a license plate fragment **“H42W.”**  
- **Annabel Miller** 👩 recognized the killer from seeing him at the gym just days earlier, on January 9, during her own workout.  

---

## 🔎 Following the Leads

Using the gym membership detail, we focused on **gold members with IDs starting with "48Z."** Cross-referencing their gym visits on January 9 and checking vehicle records for plates containing “H42W” narrowed the field dramatically. One name emerged: **Jeremy Bowers**.  

Evidence aligned perfectly: his membership, his presence at the gym that day, and his car. Jeremy Bowers was the man who pulled the trigger.  

But the story didn’t end there. When questioned, Jeremy confessed he hadn't acted alone. The real mastermind was a wealthy woman: **tallish (5'5"–5'7") with striking red hair, driving a Tesla Model S 🚗.** She had a habit of checking into the SQL Symphony Concert 🎶—three times in December 2017.  

---

## 🧩 Connecting the Dots

By tracing concert check-ins through social media, layering on physical descriptions from driver's licenses, vehicle details, and income levels 💰 to confirm wealth, the data converged on one person: **Miranda Priestly**. She orchestrated the murder and paid for it to be carried out.  

---

## ✅ Justice Through Data

With that final connection, the case closed. Jeremy Bowers was the hired hand who committed the act, but Miranda Priestly was the brains behind it all. SQL City could finally breathe easier, thanks to a relentless chase through data records that turned fragments of information into undeniable truth.
